Research

My research studies how taxation and regulation shape the behavior of individuals, firms, and digital platforms. I combine quasi-experimental methods with granular administrative and market data to identify causal effects and understand the real-world consequences of tax policy.

01

Navigating the Amazon: The Incidence of Digital Service Taxes

Dominika Langenmayr & Rohit Reddy Muddasani
Job Market Paper
Digital Markets Tax Incidence Industrial Organization

Digital Service Taxes are designed to tax digital firms in the markets where their users and customers are located. But who ultimately bears the tax? This paper studies how a digital platform responds to taxation and how the resulting costs propagate through the platform to sellers and ultimately consumers.

Data: Amazon FBA fees and product-level prices
Method: Staggered difference-in-differences
Setting: France, Italy, Spain, UK & Germany

Media Mentions

  • The Economist — “Trump threatens 50% tariffs. How might Europe strike back?” (May 23, 2025)
  • Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung — “Wie sinnvoll ist eine Digitalsteuer wirklich?” (July 19, 2025)

Policy Impact

  • Cited in a briefing prepared for the European Parliament Committee on Budgets (BUDG): Could a digital services tax become an EU own resource? (June 2026)
📄 Download Paper

Abstract

Digital firms often pay little tax in the countries where their customers are located. Digital Service Taxes (DSTs) address this issue by taxing revenues generated from users in market countries. This paper studies the incidence of DSTs using detailed data from Amazon and examines how the tax is transmitted through the platform from the platform to third-party sellers and ultimately to consumers.

Empirical Strategy

The analysis uses the staggered introduction of DSTs in France, Italy, Spain and the UK, with Germany serving as an untreated comparison country. The paper first examines the effect of DSTs on Amazon's fees to third-party sellers and then studies how changes in those fees are reflected in product prices.

02

So close and yet so far: The ability of mandatory disclosure rules to crack down on offshore tax evasion

Elisa Casi, Mohammed Mardan & Rohit Reddy Muddasani
Working Paper
Tax Evasion Tax Transparency Public Economics

Can mandatory disclosure rules actually deter offshore tax evasion—or simply redirect it? We study the introduction of DAC6 and examine how individuals respond to increased reporting requirements using bilateral data on cross-border bank deposits.

Data: Bilateral cross-border bank deposits
Policy: EU Mandatory Disclosure Rules (DAC6)
Method: Quasi-experimental policy analysis
📄 Download Paper

Abstract

This paper studies whether mandatory disclosure rules can reduce offshore tax evasion. We examine the introduction of the European Union's Directive on Administrative Cooperation (DAC6) using bilateral data on cross-border deposits.

Key Finding

Stronger enforcement is associated with reductions in deposits in affected EU countries, while some wealth appears to relocate toward jurisdictions with weaker intermediary reporting obligations. The evidence points to regulatory arbitrage rather than a complete elimination of offshore activity.

03

Survive, Heal or Die?: Zombie Firms and Tax Planning

Rohit Reddy Muddasani
Work in Progress
Corporate Taxation Multinational Firms Firm Behavior

What if some financially distressed firms survive not because they are economically viable, but because they serve a purpose within a multinational group? This paper investigates whether persistent loss-making affiliates can play a role in corporate tax planning.

Data: ORBIS Historical firm-level data
Focus: Multinational groups and distressed firms
Method: Difference-in-differences and placebo tests

Abstract

This paper investigates financially distressed firms within multinational groups and asks whether some persistent loss-making affiliates may be maintained because they facilitate corporate tax planning. The analysis compares standalone distressed firms with group-affiliated firms and examines how their behavior changes following changes in the regulatory environment.

Research Design

The study uses ORBIS Historical firm-level data and combines difference-in-differences and placebo tests to examine differences between standalone and multinational-group-affiliated firms.

Research Agenda

My broader research agenda examines taxation as a force shaping market structure, prices, firm organization, and individual behavior. I am particularly interested in the interaction between tax policy, digital markets, and globalization, and in using rigorous empirical methods and unusually detailed micro-data to uncover mechanisms that are difficult to observe in conventional economic datasets.
